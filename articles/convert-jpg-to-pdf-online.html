How to prepare photos before converting

Use clear, readable pictures

Take photos in good light and keep the camera steady. Make sure the complete document is visible, including all corners, signatures, dates and small print. Avoid glare, shadows and fingers covering the page. If a picture is blurry, converting it to PDF will not make its text clearer.

Keep the correct page sequence

For multi-page applications, arrange the images in the same order as the original papers. A useful approach is to number the image files before selecting them, then inspect the finished PDF from beginning to end. Check that no page is missing or duplicated.

Choose A4 only when it is required

A4 is a common paper size for office documents, but not every submission asks for A4. Follow the receiving portal's instructions. If a photo has a different shape from the PDF page, choose a fitting option that keeps the entire image visible rather than stretching it.

Will a JPG-to-PDF file be searchable?

A standard image-to-PDF conversion places each photo on a PDF page. The words visible inside the photograph may remain part of the image, so they may not be selectable, searchable or editable as normal text. Optical character recognition (OCR) is needed to recognise text in many scanned documents. Always check OCR output carefully because it can misread names, numbers and handwritten entries.

Does converting JPG to PDF reduce file size?

Not necessarily. A PDF made from high-resolution photographs can be large because it contains the image data. If a website or application has a file-size limit, check the finished PDF's size before uploading. You can try the QuickTools24 PDF Compressor, but compression can affect image clarity and small text. Review the result at normal viewing size before submitting it.

Can I convert JPG to PDF on an Android phone?

Yes. Open the converter in your mobile browser, choose the photos from your device's file picker and set the page options. The available memory and size of the images can affect processing speed. For a large batch of high-resolution photographs, work with a smaller group if your browser or device struggles.

Common problems and quick checks

  • Pages appear in the wrong order: return to the original images and select them in the required sequence.
  • Part of a document is cut off: check the image framing and choose a fitting option that keeps the whole picture visible.
  • Text looks too small: take a closer, sharper photo and avoid placing several document pages into one image.
  • The PDF is too large: check the image resolution and use compression carefully without making text unreadable.
  • The text cannot be selected: remember that a picture-based PDF may need a separate OCR step.
